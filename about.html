What we do

A little magic, a lot of meaning

Every one of our shows has a lesson to impart, important values, presented in a light, entertaining way.

A typical Small Wonder performance runs about 40–45 minutes and blends two or three puppet shows with a lively musical program. It's highly interactive, the kids don't just watch, they sing, laugh, and shout along the whole way through. Over 45+ years, we've honed the art of slipping a real, lasting message inside a show kids never want to end.

Along the way we've been featured in a Chanukah special on CBS TV, we've performed regularly at the Jewish Children's Museum, and for several years we headlined the Jewish Children's Expo at the Javits Center. Our shows have been praised by educators and parents coast to coast, and are warmly received across the full spectrum of Jewish observance.

A family affair

Made by one family, for yours

Small Wonder has always been a family project. At the heart of it is Rabbi Yaakov Horowitz, the voice and hands behind so many of our characters, the fellow with the guitar and the beloved old-man puppet among them. Nearly all of our scripts are written by Yaakov and his wife, the two of them shaping every story so it lands with a laugh and leaves something behind.

From there it grew the way the best things do: everyone pitched in. Children, in-laws, and now grandchildren have all taken a turn behind the stage, learning the voices, running the shows, and keeping the whole thing feeling homemade. Three generations on, the person setting up in your room is almost always family.

Handmade, one at a time

Nearly all of our puppets are made by hand by remarkably talented people in the community, usually a one-time labor of love. As one maker joked when he finally finished: "It took so long, I can't possibly charge you what it's worth. So here's your puppet, but don't ever call me again!"

A stage that travels

Our stage was designed more than thirty years ago out of copper tubing, hinged so the whole thing folds open in seconds. We arrive completely self-contained, stage, sound, and lighting, set up in minutes, and bring the whole world of the show right to your room.

The heart of it

One of our oldest and most beloved shows, The Unhappy King, has been performed thousands of times. Its message is simple: true happiness comes from making someone else happy. Years ago, a woman stopped us just to say that show had changed her life. "You taught me, if you want to be happy, make someone else happy. Now I look for a way to do that every single day." That's why we do this.

Meet Uncle Pinchy

And then there's Uncle Pinchy, a longtime friend of the theater, and these days the face of our move into ventriloquism. (We even had a full-body ventriloquist figure of him built by a puppet maker up in Toronto, with his permission, of course.)
